## LinkedIn

A physician with $2 million in retirement assets and a $400,000 mortgage needs a very different insurance profile than a retiree living on Social Security and a pension. Sell both the same umbrella policy and one is overpaying for coverage they'll never use, while the other stays dangerously exposed.

Our new post walks through a risk-based way to size coverage: start with a real net worth statement, calculate income replacement by years to retirement (not a flat salary multiple), then layer on business-specific policies like malpractice or cyber liability based on your profession. It also covers why a $200-400/year umbrella policy is the most underused tool for protecting assets you've already built.

If you're a physician, dentist, real estate professional, or retiree in Southeast Texas rethinking your coverage, start here: https://www.bblcpa.com/resources/insuring-where-it-counts-avoid-over-insuring-in-the-wrong-pl

## Facebook

Standard homeowners and auto policies usually cap liability at $300,000 to $500,000, a limit that made sense in 1985 but not for a family with a rental property, a boat, or a teenage driver today. A dentist earning $280,000 a year with 20 years left in practice is carrying roughly $5 million in future earning power, far more than a basic term policy covers. Our latest post breaks down how to size your coverage around your real net worth and cash flow instead of a generic checklist.
