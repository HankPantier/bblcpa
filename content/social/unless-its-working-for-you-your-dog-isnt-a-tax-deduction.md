## LinkedIn

A guard dog watching over a vacant listing between closings can be a real deduction. A Labrador who lives at your home office in Nederland is not.

The IRS draws a hard line in Publication 535: a dog has to serve a documented business purpose, not just keep you company, to count as an ordinary and necessary expense. Guide dogs get a separate path under Publication 502, deductible as a medical expense, but only once your total qualifying costs clear the 7.5% AGI floor and you itemize.

This matters most for real estate professionals with guard dogs on vacant properties, veterinarians with working dogs on staff, and retirees claiming a service animal alongside Medicare premiums and RMDs. The deduction rarely moves your tax bill on its own, but it's exactly the kind of detail that gets missed without someone looking at the whole return.
